1.          Article I: Purpose and Scope

## 1.1      Purpose

The CMMC Code of Professional Conduct (CoPC) establishes the ethical and
                     professional standards required for participants operating within the CMMC
                     Ecosystem, as well as the procedures for investigating and adjudicating violations
                     of the CoPC. It also provides guidance for CMMC Ecosystem participants on
                     navigating prospective conflicts-of-interest and other impartiality issues that might
                     arise while conducting CMMC-related activities.
                     This CoPC represents the professional performance standards to which the
                     members of the CMMC Ecosystem will be held accountable and the procedures
                     for addressing violations of those standards.2

## 1.2      Covered Persons and Entities

The CoPC applies to all individuals, entities, and groups operating within the
                     CMMC Ecosystem, to include:

                     ▪    The Cyber AB, including its professional staff and Board of Directors
                     ▪    The CAICO, including its professional staff
                     ▪    CMMC Third-Party Assessment Organizations (C3PAOs)3
                     ▪    CMMC Certified Assessors (CCAs)
                     ▪    CMMC Certified Professionals (CCPs)
                     ▪    CMMC Certified Instructors (CCIs) and Provisional Instructors (PIs)
                     ▪    CMMC Approved Publishing Partners (APPs)
                     ▪    CMMC Approved Training Providers (ATPs)
                     ▪    CMMC Registered Practitioners (RPs)
                     ▪    CMMC Registered Practitioners Advanced (RPAs)
                     ▪    CMMC Registered Practitioner Organizations (RPOs)
                     ▪    CAICO Academic Advisory Council (AAC)

                     Organizations Seeking Assessment (OSAs) and Organizations Seeking
                     Certification (OSCs) are not bound to the CoPC but are encouraged to adopt its
                     practices, where applicable. Department of Defense personnel and contractors
                     involved in CMMC activities remain subject to Federal Government ethics
                     regulations and contractual obligations, respectively, and are not governed by the
                     CoPC.

    2.      Article II: Guiding Principles

            The CoPC is built upon a foundation of fundamental guiding principles:

            ▪     Professionalism: conducting activities with honesty, fairness, and respect for others.
            ▪     Impartiality: avoiding conflicts of interest and maintaining unbiased decision-making.
            ▪     Confidentiality: protecting sensitive data and proprietary information.
            ▪     Information Integrity: ensuring the accuracy and security of information.
            ▪     Lawful and Ethical Behavior: complying with all applicable laws and regulations.
            ▪     Equal Opportunity: promoting inclusivity and refraining from discriminatory behavior.
            ▪     Proper Use of CMMC Methods: employing CMMC processes and procedures.
            ▪     Proper Use of Technology and AI: incorporating technology into CMMC appropriately.

            The CoPC practices are derived from these fundamental principles and are to be regarded
            as mandatory professional standards. All participants within the CMMC Ecosystem are
            expected to uphold these principles and practices in all activities that relate to carrying out
            their roles within the CMMC Program.

                      awarded DoD contracts, hang in the balance within the CMMC Program. This

                      times.

                      b) Being honest and factual in all dealings with colleagues, clients, trainees, and

                   confidence that they will be treated fairly, impartially, without bias, and that others in

                   personal, financial, or other pressures that could compromise the impartiality of
                   CMMC assessments and certifications. Impartiality is the core of what instills trust
                   and confidence in the CMMC Program.

                   a) Disclosing and mitigating conflicts of interest (COI) in a proactive and timely

                      known, or reasonably should be known, that would compromise the impartiality
                      of an impending or ongoing Level 2 certification assessment.6

                      appendices.
                   c) Avoiding participation in any activity, practice, or transaction that could result in
                      an actual or perceived conflict of interest.7

                      certification process for an assessment in which they previously served as

## 2.3        Confidentiality

All CMMC Ecosystem members should respect, protect, and maintain the
                      confidentiality of other parties’ data. In carrying out CMMC activities, Ecosystem
                      members may be made aware of certain confidential information that is acquired in
                      the performance of professional services. This information might include, but is not
                      necessarily limited to, proprietary data, trade secrets, business strategies, security
                      postures, and personal information that may be contained within various information
                      systems. CMMC Ecosystem members must treat confidential information with the
                      utmost care, and under no circumstances reveal information learned during the
                      delivery of CMMC services to anyone who is not expressly authorized to view it.
                      CMMC Confidentiality includes the following practices:

                      a) Maintaining the confidentiality of customer and government data to preclude
                         unauthorized disclosure.9
                      b) Exercising due care to ensure that confidential or privileged information
                         gathered during CMMC assessments or consulting engagements remains so,
                         even after the work has ended.
                      c) Refraining from copying or storing proprietary information or materials from
                         external entities without explicit permission to do so.
                      d) Forgoing making premature assertions or declarations about outcomes of
                         assessments, training classes, exams, or other results-based CMMC activities.

## 2.7     Proper Use of CMMC Methods

Uniform processes and procedures are paramount to the attainment of consistency
                in CMMC assessments and CMMC training courses. While some measure of
                discretion is granted to the judgement and experience of CMMC Certified Assessors
                (CCAs) and CMMC Certified Instructors (CCIs), the backbone of CMMC consistency
                is the unwavering reliance upon, and employment of, officially approved processes,
                procedures, methodologies, and curricula.
                The Proper Use of CMMC Methods includes the following practices:
                a) Relying upon, and adhering to, the authoritative CMMC references of 32 CFR

# Part 170; NIST Special Publication 800-171 Revision 2, “Protecting Controlled

Unclassified Information in Nonfederal Systems and Organizations”, and NIST
                   Special Publication 800-171A, “Assessing Security Requirements for Controlled
                   Unclassified Information”.
                b) For C3PAOs, adhering to the procedural requirements and guidance contained
                   within the CMMC Assessment Process (CAP).
                c) Participating actively within, and respecting the boundaries of, the role you are
                   executing, and the responsibilities contained therein (e.g., CMMC Certified
                   Professionals (CCPs) shall not encroach upon the responsibilities of a CCA
                   during a CMMC assessment).
                d) Teaching CMMC certification courses only with approved CMMC training
                   content developed by a CMMC Approved Publishing Partner (APP).

## 2.8 Proper Use of Technology and Artificial Intelligence

All CMMC Ecosystem members must ensure the responsible and ethical use of
                artificial intelligence (AI) and other emerging technologies in their conduct of CMMC
                activities.
                The CMMC Proper Use of Technology and AI includes the following practices:
                a) Avoiding use of AI or automated assessment technology that renders
                   subservient or diminishes the authority and autonomy of CCAs in a CMMC
                   certification assessment.
                b) Avoiding biases in AI systems and algorithms used for assessment preparation
                   and assessment conduct.
                c) Prohibiting providing customer data to an Internet-accessible AI application.
                d) Ensuring transparency in technology employment in CMMC activities.
                e) Upholding data privacy and security when employing technology solutions.

3.      Article III: Conflicts of Interest

                                                  10

        A conflict of interest (COI) is the greatest threat to the compromise of impartiality in CMMC
        assessments and activities.

## 3.1     Definition

A conflict of interest is a situation in which an individual has competing and incompatible
        relationships, obligations, or affiliations with two different parties, in which the goals, aims,
        or concerns of those parties are inherently at odds. A conflict of interest arises when a
        participant’s personal, financial, or professional relationships compromise or appear to
        compromise their impartiality. Conflicts of interest can compromise good judgment and
        undermine trust and confidence in institutions and systems, including the CMMC Program.

## 3.2     Disclosure and Mitigation

There is nothing intrinsically unethical about a COI. Over the course of a career, modern
        professionals hold employment in numerous companies and organizations, invest in various
        business concerns, have families, and develop scores of personal friendships. Rather, it is
        the failure to disclose a COI that runs afoul of responsible ethical behavior. Most COIs can
        be mitigated in some manner or another, while other conflicts must simply be avoided based
        on their intractable nature. The transparency of disclosed conflicts of interest goes a long
        way toward insulating the individual from scrutiny and potential charges of ethical
        misconduct.
        Disclosure and mitigation/avoidance of COI are critical to the success of CMMC. Without
        these, trust and confidence in the CMMC Ecosystem cannot be achieved.

## 3.3     CMMC Conflicts of Interest

The primary CMMC COI that poses the greatest threat to the impartiality of CMMC—and
        specifically to the trust and confidence of a CMMC certification assessment—is that which
        exists between the C3PAO (including, most importantly, individuals participating on its
        Assessment Team) and the Organization Seeking Certification that is to be assessed. 32
        CFR §170.8(b)(17)(ii)(G) establishes the overarching conflict that is to be avoided:

              “Prohibit CMMC Ecosystem members from participating in the Level 2 certification
              process for an assessment in which they previously served as a consultant to
              prepare the organization for any CMMC assessment within 3 years.”

        This prohibition applies to the C3PAO as an organization as well as to all of its
        Assessment Team members. It covers any preparatory, advisory, or consulting activities
        for any type of CMMC assessment. For example, an individual who served as a
        consultant to an organization preparing for a CMMC Level 1 self-assessment two years
        ago would be precluded from serving on a C3PAO Assessment Team performing a
        CMMC Level 2 certification assessment until the three-year prohibition term had expired.
        The consulting/advisory conflict one of several COIs that require disclosure and potential
        mitigation/avoidance measures between the C3PAO and an OSC. C3PAO conflicts of
        interest—both organizational and individual—could be based on financial, business,
        familial, or other relationships, such as:

                                                   11

                ▪   Financial interest in the OSC;
                ▪   Business partnership or teaming relationship with the OSC;
                ▪   Prior employment by the OSC; and
                ▪   Family connection or close friendship with the OSC.

        These types of relationships with the OSC would require disclosure, but depending on the
        particulars of each situation, they may or may not necessarily constitute a COI that could not
        be mitigated. Each COI situation is a specific use-case all its own with unique details and
        circumstances. The health of the CMMC Ecosystem relies on each member understanding
        what a conflict of interest is, what needs to be disclosed, and how best it can be either
        mitigated or avoided.
        In addition to the COI requirements outlined in 32 CFR part 170, C3PAOs must also comply
        with the impartiality responsibilities contained in Section 4.1. of ISO/IEC 17020:2012,
        “Conformity Assessment—Requirements for the operation of various types of bodies
        performing inspection.” This section provides additional information on the types of
        relationships that might threaten the C3PAOs impartiality. C3PAOs are required to identify
        COI and risks to their impartiality on an ongoing basis
        It is the responsibility of the C3PAO alone to ensure COI are disclosed and either
        mitigated or avoided in ensure the impartiality of the CMMC Level 2 certification
        assessment.
        Neither The Cyber AB nor the CAICO will provide impartiality advice or issue a COI “opinion”
        to a C3PAO or any member of an Assessment Team.
        Examples of hypothetical COI scenarios and their prospective resolution are provided for
        reference in Appendix A.
        The Cyber AB will enforce the above rules and take action against any confirmed violations
        in accordance with The Cyber AB’s enforcement policy outlined in Article IV.

## 3.4     Non-Certification Assessments

A non-certification assessment is the conduct by a C3PAO of a full or partial cybersecurity
        assessment of an OSA that does not result in the issuance or denial of a formal certification.
        Non-certification assessments are often referred to as “mock assessments,” “gap
        assessments,” or “dry-run assessments,” among others. A non-certification assessment
        could involve the conduct by a C3PAO of a full or partial CMMC assessment or assessments
        of conformity to other standards (e.g., ISO/IEC 27001:2022).
        A non-certification assessment does not result in the issuance of a CMMC Certificate of
        Status nor convey any standing within the CMMC Program. Non-certification assessments
        are conducted outside the purview of the CMMC Program and are not reported to the CMMC
        instantiation of eMASS.
        Some OSAs may elect for a C3PAO to conduct a non-certification assessment that will serve
        as the basis of a CMMC self-assessment reporting responsibility they may have. Others may
        choose a non-certification assessment to solicit a formal, structured third-party evaluation of
        the effectiveness of their CMMC implementation to date.
        To be regarded as a true mock assessment that does not create a conflict of interest or the

                                                     12

        risk to impartiality as established in 32 CFR §170.8(b)(17)(ii)(G) as described in Section 3.3
        above, the C3PAO must meet the following conditions:

              a) the non-certification assessment shall be conducted in a formal fashion and in
                 accordance with the assessment procedures established in 32 CFR Part 170 and
                 the relevant non-certification, non-reporting aspects of the CMMC Assessment
                 Process (CAP), or to the conformity requirements of other cybersecurity
                 standards;
              b) the C3PAO shall not provide any recommendations, advice, or consultative
                 information as to how the OSA might remediate any discrepancies or improve their
                 security posture for an official CMMC assessment or for conformity to any other
                 cybersecurity standard; and
              a) the OSA shall receive a deliverable documenting the official results of the non-
                 certification assessment.

        The C3PAO shall retain the results deliverables of all non-certification assessments
        conducted for the prior three (3) years. The results deliverables shall be made available to
        The Cyber AB, upon reasonable request, for purposes of review during a CMMC
        authorization renewal or accreditation assessment of the C3PAO by The Cyber AB.

4. Article IV: Enforcement
        This Code of Professional Conduct, essential to maintaining trust and confidence in the
        CMMC Ecosystem, will be actively enforced by The Cyber AB pursuant to its responsibilities
        under 32 CFR §170.8(b)(17)(ii)(B), DoD contract #HQ003430H0003, and ISO/IEC
        17011:2017. The following procedures apply to the inquiry, review, adjudication, and
        enforcement of the CoPC.

## 4.1     Reporting Violations

The Cyber AB monitors the CMMC-related activity of all CMMC authorized, accredited,
        certified, designated, approved, and registered individuals and organizations within the
        Ecosystem and reserves the right to investigate any potential violations of this Code that
        arise from questionable behavior. In addition, the Department of Defense plays a role in
        identifying and reporting potential violations that come to its attention.
        The size and scale of the CMMC Ecosystem, however, presents challenges for The Cyber
        AB and DoD to effectively monitor all members and activities by themselves. All members
        of the CMMC Ecosystem are encouraged to engage in the “self-regulating” culture of the
        Program in furtherance of maintaining trust and confidence in the CMMC Program.
        When observing other individuals or organizations within the CMMC Ecosystem making
        choices that may be in violation of the CoPC, interested parties should consider privately
        requesting clarification or offering to assist in rectifying the alleged violation. However, if
        clarification or resolution is not attainable, or if an individual believes corrective action is
        required to resolve the situation, then the individual may submit a complaint report to The
        Cyber AB.

         Reports of potential CMMC violations may be submitted to The Cyber AB by sending an
         email to complaints@cyberab.org.

## 4.2      Investigation and Adjudication

Upon receipt of a complaint or suggestion of a potential violation of the CoPC, The Cyber
          AB Compliance Officer will initiate a formal case designation and begin a fact-finding inquiry
          in accordance with its documented process for receiving, evaluating, and rendering
          decisions on complaints.
          The Cyber AB’s formal Complaint Process is available on its website, www.cyberab.org.
          Based on the complaint and subsequent fact-finding inquiry, the Compliance Officer will
          determine if a formal investigation of the matter is warranted. If a formal investigation is
          initiated, The Cyber AB will inform DoD in writing of such within 72 hours.16
          The Compliance Officer will conduct a thorough investigation into the matter in accordance
          with procedures that provide notice to the accused, an opportunity to respond, and review
          by unbiased decisionmakers with a right of appeal.
          Upon completion of the investigation, the Compliance Officer will submit the results to The
          Cyber AB’s Ethics and Compliance Committee of its Board of Directors. The Ethics and
          Compliance Committee will review the case file and determine if additional information is
          required. Upon final adjudication, the Ethics and Compliance Committee will render a
          decision on the validity of the original complaint and any penalties that might be imposed,
          pursuant to Complaint Process.
          The Cyber AB will report to DoD in writing the outcome of completed investigations within

# 15 business days.17

## 4.3      Corrective Action and Penalties

The investigation may result in findings and recommendations for corrective action and/or
         penalties. Actions may include warning, remediation, suspension, or denial or termination of
         CMMC credentials, as well as temporary or permanent loss of eligibility for such credentials.
         The Cyber AB and the CAICO have the sole authority to determine the action to be taken. In
         the event of termination of credentials, the termination will be conducted in accordance with
         the provisions of The Cyber AB’s Complaint Process.

## 4.4      Appeals

Individuals and organization subject to The Cyber AB’s Ethics and Compliance Committee
         decisions will have 21 days from the date of the report of the decision to file an appeal.
         Appeals will be received, considered, and adjudicated in accordance with The Cyber AB’s
         Appeals Process.
         The Cyber AB Appeals Process is available on www.cyberab.org.

          information security officer (CISO). Unaware of this relationship, the C3PAO assigns Larry
          to be a member of the Assessment Team for this Level 2 certification assessment.
          Conflict: If Larry were to proceed with participating in this Level 2 certification assessment,
          his requirement for impartiality would conflict with his relationship with his brother. Left
          unmitigated, Larry’s impartiality on the assessment would be compromised based on his
          likely sentiment for favoring a positive assessment outcome for Darrel and his company (or,
          in the event of a contentious brotherly relationship, possibly favoring a negative outcome).
          Mitigation: When made aware of his assignment to be part of this Assessment Team, Larry
          discloses the conflict of interest to the C3PAO. Upon Larry’s disclosure, the C3PAO
          mitigates the conflict by removing him from the Assessment Team for that C3PAO and
          replaces Larry with another CCA. Since there is no longer this or any other conflict in play,
          the C3PAO proceeds with the certification assessment.
          Additional Discussion: Even if Larry qualified his disclosure that he and his brother had grown
          apart over the years due to them living on opposite coasts and that they had not seen or
          spoken with each other in years, the appearance of a COI would still exist and likely require
          similar mitigation on the part of the C3PAO.

          a specialized set of CMMC conformance templates that assist companies in organizing their

        Example A.3: Financial COI Between C3PAO and OSC
        Scenario: Joanna is the chief operating officer of an Accredited C3PAO. She is neither a
        CCA nor CCP, but she sits on the C3PAO’s Appeals Committee. Long ago, at the advice of
        her financial advisor, she invested in a publicly traded company that designs and
        manufactures advanced avionics for both commercial and U.S. military aircraft. Her
        investment is in the form of direct ownership of the company’s common stock. That same
        company has recently approached the C3PAO about conducting its CMMC Level 2
        certification assessment. Joanna had previously disclosed her financial interest in this
        company as part of the C3PAO’s internal COI disclosure program. When the C3PAO’s
        compliance manager conducted the COI screening before taking on the new client, Joanna’s
        disclosure was identified.
        Conflict: The conflict exists between Joanna’s financial interest in the avionics company and
        the C3PAO’s responsibilities to conduct an impartial CMMC Level 2 certification
        assessment, including any potential appeal of the assessment results.
        Mitigation: Since Joanna is neither a CCA nor a CCP and is not directly involved in the
        C3PAO’s cybersecurity compliance business line, there is no direct COI with the
        Assessment Team and this OSC. However, as COO, Joanna does sit on the C3PAO’s
        Appeals Committee. The C3PAO mitigates this conflict by Joanna recusing herself from any
        involvement or influence is this particular OSC’s assessment process or any potential appeal
        or complaint that might emerge from it. This recusal is documented by the C3PAO in a
        memorandum for the record and is retained as part of the assessment file for this OSC.

# Appendix B: CMMC Position-Specific Professional Responsibilities

Within the CMMC Ecosystem, individuals may hold multiple roles. Each role held by an individual,
however, has position-specific professional and ethical requirements to which adherence is
mandatory. Violating these requirements could result in referral to the CMMC Accreditation Body
and/or the CAICO for inquiry, review, and adjudication, including the possible imposition of
penalties.

        B.1 Assessors

        The following professional responsibilities apply to all Assessor-related cadres within the
        CMMC Ecosystem, to include CMMC Certified Assessors (CCAs), CMMC Certified
        Professionals (CCPs), and Lead Assessors.
        Professional responsibilities of Assessors include:

            a) Participate on a CMMC Assessment only under the direct employment or contract
               of an Authorized or Accredited C3PAO;
            b) Complete CCA or CCP training as delivered only by an Approved Training
               Provider (ATP) that utilizes curriculum developed by an Approved Publishing
               Partner (APP) and that has provided CMMC Approved Training Materials (CATM).
               All ATPs, APPs, and CATM must be approved by the CAICO;
            c) Maintain up-to-date training and certification per CAICO requirements;
            d) Render annual professional maintenance fees to the CAICO;
            e) Sign annual renewal agreements with the CAICO;
            f)   Disclose all relevant conflicts of interest, including where an appearance thereof
                 may exist, to any company or organization employing or contracting you to
                 participate in a CMMC assessment;
            g) Refrain from knowingly and intentionally disseminating disinformation about the
               CMMC Program, other members of the CMMC Ecosystem, The Cyber AB, and/or
               the CAICO;
            h) Provide all documentation and records in English;
            i)   Do not share any CMMC assessment-related outcomes or advanced information
                 with any person not assigned to that specific assessment, except as otherwise
                 required by law.

            j)   Immediately notify the responsible C3PAO of any breach or potential breach of
                 security to any CMMC-related assessment materials under the assessor’s
                 purview; and
             k) Only use IT, cloud, cybersecurity services, and end-point devices provided by the
                authorized/accredited C3PAO that has been engaged to perform that OSC’s Level

                 assessment-related information. The evaluation of assessment evidence within the
